# Paper 1: abstract and claims draft (English, 15 Nov 2026; every number is in REVIEW.md)

**Working title.** Importance scores for chain-of-thought steps do not predict what a model needs to be trained on, and SFT estimates of "reasoning capacity per written step" depend on the training budget.

**Abstract (draft).** Methods that prune chains of thought score each step with a causal or information-theoretic measure (probability of necessity, leave-one-out, step entropy) and drop the lowest-scoring steps. We validate these scores against ground truth planted in the chain and test the pruning they imply by supervised fine-tuning of 1.5B to 7B models on GSM8K. (i) Leave-one-out separates planted redundant steps from load-bearing ones best (AUROC 0.66 to 0.81), probability of necessity is biased only for the 1.5B model, and step entropy ranks below chance. (ii) Fine-tuning on pruned chains loses 5 to 46 points of accuracy for every score tested, and the accuracy change over 59 pruned arms is explained by how many original steps each kept step must absorb (r = −0.86), not by the score; deleting the most important steps is not worse than deleting the least important ones at 3B and below (+9.3 points in favour of the score at 7B). The loss is not reduced by three times the training data (random deletion: −24.0 and −33.4 points against −22.6 and −39.4). (iii) In synthetic chains with a known minimal length (pointer chasing), the number of lookups a model can compose in one written step looks like a sharp knee at 3 to 4 lookups under a small fine-tuning budget, but the apparent knee moves with the budget: Qwen3-1.7B at 8 lookups per step goes from 0.31 to 0.64 to 0.99 accuracy over three successive triplings of the data, and final loss separates solved from unsolved runs. We conclude that capacity statements obtained by SFT must name the budget and the convergence status. (iv) Independent of fine-tuning, prefilled models copy a wrong stated final result on 99.5% (1.5B), 85.5% (7B), 66% (14B) and 77% (32B, different 4-bit source) of questions they solve themselves.

**Claims that can be made, with their limits.**
1. Score validation against planted ground truth (planted duplicates; load-bearing steps from the number-dependency graph). Limit: one task family (GSM8K), planted steps are lexically marked.
2. Pruning loses accuracy whatever the score; matched-token random deletion is as good as the scores. Limit: SFT at ~500–1800 chains and 2–3 epochs, one seed per cell, noise 3–4 points between runs.
3. The effective stride of the training chains explains the loss better than the token ratio. Limit: post hoc, arms differ in several respects at once.
4. SFT-estimated capacity per written step depends on budget and seed (3B stride 4: 0.57 to 0.91 over five seeds at 3000 × 2). Limit: synthetic tasks, one family of tasks per claim.
5. Copy rate of a wrong stated result against model size. Limit: only questions the model solves (selection gap measured on another task: −12.6 points at 7B); 14B and 32B in 4-bit.

**What is not claimed.** That a model has a capacity of k lookups per written step; that any pruning method improves a model; anything about frontier models (no frontier run yet).
