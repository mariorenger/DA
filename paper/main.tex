\begin{abstract}
When a language model writes a reasoning chain, does its answer depend on that chain, and through
which path? We study this with interventions whose ground truth is known by construction:
inserted redundant hops (true necessity $0$), programmatic input changes (true effect $0$), and an
exact mediation analysis in which the chain is set directly. We report four results on two
multi-hop datasets with five models from two families (Qwen2.5 0.5B--7B, SmolLM2-1.7B).
\textbf{(i) Step masking is blind to bridge reasoning.} Masking one intermediate hop changes
$p(\text{gold})$ by at most $6.5$ points, whereas masking it together with its descendants in the
chain's reasoning graph changes it by $41$--$77$ points; we show this is an identity, not noise
(single-step masking estimates a controlled direct effect, which vanishes on bridge chains).
\textbf{(ii) Resampling-based necessity with a fixed gold baseline is biased by the generator.}
Probability-of-necessity estimates computed against the original chain assign $56$--$78$ points of
necessity to provably redundant steps; the bias equals one minus the rate at which the model can
regenerate a correct continuation, and a resampled baseline removes it ($-0.3$ to $4.3$ points).
\textbf{(iii) Chains are read, not reasoned over.} Answers depend on the chain when the answer is a
string written in it ($41$--$77$ points) and barely when it must be composed from it ($-1.5$ to
$7.3$ points at every scale). A binary intervention that appends a \emph{wrong} stated
answer is followed $46$--$67\%$ of the time up to 3B---as often on questions the model solves from
the plain chain as on those it does not---while Qwen2.5-7B follows it on only $10\%$ of the
questions it can solve. \textbf{(iv) Distractors harm chain-of-thought through the chain.} With
the chain held at its original value, the effect of four distractor paragraphs on CoT vanishes
(direct effect $-0.6$ to $+1.0$ EM, $n{=}500$), while direct answering is harmed directly ($-3.8$ to
$-4.6$). A readout-level decoding probe that contrasts the model with interventions on its own hops
therefore cannot help, and does not. \textbf{(v) Executing the composition outside the model does.}
When the model only extracts facts and genders and a rule table induced from training data composes
them along the kinship graph, CLUTRR accuracy rises by $21$--$37$ points over compute-matched
self-consistency on short chains (1.5B--7B, two unseen splits) and by $24$ points on unseen long
chains at 7B; given the same extracted facts, the model itself is no better than CoT. All hypotheses
were fixed before each GPU run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Chain-of-thought prompting~\citep{wei2022cot} makes models write intermediate steps, but writing a
step is not the same as \emph{using} it. Causal tests show that models frequently ignore or
post-rationalise their chains~\citep{lanham2023faithfulness,turpin2023unfaithful,paul2024frodo,
tutek2025fur}, and that step importance depends on how a step is removed~\citep{bogdan2025anchors,
macar2026branches}. What these studies lack is ground truth: without knowing which steps are
necessary, an estimator of step importance cannot be validated, only compared with other
estimators.

We build that ground truth. On CLUTRR~\citep{sinha2019clutrr} (kinship graphs) and
MuSiQue~\citep{trivedi2022musique} (real multi-hop questions with gold decompositions) we write gold
reasoning chains, insert one true but irrelevant hop, and ask each estimator to separate necessary
from redundant hops. We pair this with binary input interventions whose effect on the answer is
zero by construction, placebo and mask-token controls, and an exact mediation analysis in which the
chain itself is set, so that direct and indirect effects are observed per question rather than
estimated.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{A ground-truth protocol for chain reliance}: necessity labels and null effects known
by construction; placebo, mask-marker and volume-matched controls; exact NDE/TIE mediation.
\item \textbf{Two estimator biases, with closed forms.} Single-step masking measures a controlled
direct effect that vanishes on bridge chains (\cref{prop:direct}); resampling necessity against a
fixed gold continuation adds $1-r$, where $r$ is the model's regeneration success
(\cref{prop:rollout}). Both are confirmed at every scale we test.
\item \textbf{Read, not reasoned.} Reliance on the chain appears when the answer can be
copied from it and is near zero when it must be composed; a single binary intervention
($T_{\text{copy}}$) switches it on. Its wrong-answer variant separates copying from the inability
to compose: up to 3B a stated wrong answer overrides the model's own solution; at 7B it is mostly
vetoed, yet the relational hops still carry no measurable effect.
\item \textbf{Where robustness failures live.} Distractors harm chain-of-thought entirely through
the regenerated chain, which explains why readout-level contrastive decoding---including a
graph-aware rule we formulate as a probe (\hgcd{})---cannot help at this scale.
\item \textbf{A decoding intervention the diagnosis predicts.} Moving the composition out of the
model (extract facts, execute the reasoning graph's structural equations with a rule table) beats
compute-matched self-consistency by $13$--$37$ points, and an ablation holding the extracted facts
fixed shows the gain is caused by the composition step (\cref{sec:decoding}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Faithfulness of reasoning chains.}
\citet{lanham2023faithfulness} intervene on model-written chains (truncation, inserted mistakes,
paraphrases); \citet{turpin2023unfaithful} show biased features steer answers without being
verbalised; FRODO~\citep{paul2024frodo} uses causal mediation~\citep{vig2020mediation} and trains
small models with counterfactual preferences; FUR~\citep{tutek2025fur} measures faithfulness by
unlearning steps; Thought Anchors~\citep{bogdan2025anchors} measures step importance by
counterfactual resampling, and Thought Branches~\citep{macar2026branches} argues that step
effects must be measured by resampling the continuation (a total effect), noting that removing a
sentence while preventing its content from reappearing changes conclusions. FRODO already treats
the rationale as a mediator and finds weak indirect effects for models under 20B, and
\citet{somov2026breaking} find that models often fail to update after edits to intermediate
structures. Our contribution relative to these is narrower and specific: (i) necessity labels and
null effects that are \emph{known by construction}, so step-importance estimators can be
\emph{validated} rather than compared with each other; (ii) a deterministic, rollout-free
total-effect intervention for hop-structured chains, validated against resampling; (iii) the
bridge-versus-compositional distinction, which predicts exactly where direct-effect estimators
fail; and (iv) an exact mediation of distractor effects (chain vs.\ readout).

\paragraph{Necessity and sufficiency.} \citet{yu2025pns} define step-level probabilities of
necessity and sufficiency for chain-of-thought, prove identifiability under exogeneity and
monotonicity (building on \citet{tian2000probabilities,pearl2009causality}), and use PNS to prune
chains for in-context learning and fine-tuning. We show the same quantity arises as the limit of a
decoding rule, and we separate direct from total effects~\citep{pearl2001direct}.

\paragraph{Contrastive and guided decoding.} Contrastive decoding~\citep{li2023contrastive,
obrien2023cdreasoning}, DoLa~\citep{chuang2024dola}, CFG~\citep{sanchez2024cfg}, CAD and its
adaptive/gated variants~\citep{shi2024cad,wang2025adacad,tao2026nwcad} contrast distributions under
different models, layers or contexts. Visual contrastive decoding~\citep{leng2024vcd} uses a
\emph{distorted} rather than empty input; our L1 variant is the textual analogue with a
type-matched counterfactual. Pced~\citep{corallo2026pced} treats each retrieved document as an
expert contrasted with a no-context baseline; RED~\citep{yamaguchi2026red} multiplies
image- and rationale-conditional distributions in vision-language models. None of these intervene on
the hops of the model's own reasoning graph, distinguish direct from total effects, or relate the
contrast to PNS.

\paragraph{Causal analyses of reasoning.} \citet{stolfo2023causal} quantify robustness of
mathematical reasoning through interventions on a causal graph of the input;
\citet{zecevic2023parrots} argue models ``talk'' causality without being causal; text-intervention
effect estimation~\citep{guo2024causaldann} uses LLM rewrites as treatments. Our interventions are
programmatic, so their ground-truth effects are known exactly.

\section{Setup: a Structural Causal Model of Multi-Hop Reasoning}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Variables.} A question $Q$, a context $P=(P_{\mathrm{sup}},P_{\mathrm{dist}})$ of
supporting and distractor passages, a chain of hops $H_1,\dots,H_k$, and an answer $A$. Each hop
is rendered as \texttt{Hop $i$: $\langle$body$\rangle$ => $\langle$result$\rangle$}. The model acts
as a \emph{reader} mechanism $\pa(a\mid Q,P,h_{1:k})$ over a finite candidate set $\mathcal{A}$.

\paragraph{Two task graphs.} In \emph{bridge} chains (MuSiQue), hop $j$ resolves an entity that
the next hop consumes and the answer is the result of the last hop:
$Q\to H_1\to\cdots\to H_k\to A$, with $P_{\mathrm{sup},j}\to H_j$. In \emph{compositional} chains
(CLUTRR), every hop contributes a relation that is composed into the answer:
$H_j\to A$ for all $j$ and $H_j\to H_{j+1}$ through the shared entity. Distractors have no edge to
$A$ in either task graph (\cref{fig:scm}).

\paragraph{Directed reasoning graph (DRG).} Given a generated chain $y=(h_1,\dots,h_k)$ we add an
edge $i\to j$ ($i<j$) if the result of $h_i$ is mentioned in the body of $h_j$. Terminal hops are
those whose result is the query target (CLUTRR) or the last hop (MuSiQue); the \emph{relevant set}
$\Rel(y)$ contains the terminal hops and their DRG ancestors. Hops outside $\Rel(y)$ have no
directed path to the answer.

\paragraph{Interventions on hops.}
\begin{definition}[Naive and graph-aware interventions]
$\donai{j}$ replaces hop $j$ by a mask token and leaves all other hops unchanged.
$\dotot{j}$ masks hop $j$ and, in every DRG descendant $d$ of $j$, masks every mention of the
results of $j$ and of $d$. The latter is a deterministic, rollout-free approximation of
$\mathrm{do}(H_j{=}\bot)$: descendant values are functions of $h_j$, hence unknown under the
intervention. A \emph{placebo} rewrites hop $j$ without changing its entities or relation.
\end{definition}
For an answer $a$ we write $\Delta^{\mathrm{nai}}_j(a)=\pa(a\mid y)-\pa(a\mid\donai{j})$ and
$\Delta^{\mathrm{tot}}_j(a)=\pa(a\mid y)-\pa(a\mid\dotot{j})$, suppressing $Q,P$.

\paragraph{What is (and is not) causal here.} We do not assume the language model \emph{is} an SCM
of the task. The reader $\pa$ is a deterministic function of its input, so editing hops in the
prompt is an exact intervention on the reader's input, and $\Delta_j$ is an exact (not estimated)
effect \emph{on the reader}. The task SCM only supplies ground truth: which hops are necessary,
and which input changes should have no effect on the answer.

\paragraph{Mask-token artifacts.} Replacing text by \texttt{[MASKED]} is itself out of
distribution. Our estimator benchmark controls for this: the inserted redundant hop is masked in
exactly the same way, so any effect caused by the mask token alone appears as a non-zero
$\Delta$ on redundant hops and lowers the AUC. In addition, a \emph{mask-marker} control inserts
\texttt{[MASKED]} into hop $j$ while keeping all of its content; its effect
$\Delta^{\mathrm{marker}}_j$ measures the mask-token artifact directly and should be $\approx0$.
Because $\dotot{j}$ masks a whole subtree, we also use a \emph{volume-matched} control that inserts
the same mask tokens into hop $j$ and every descendant while keeping all of their content, and a
counterfactual-replacement version of the total effect that uses no mask on hop $j$ at all.

\section{Theory: two estimator biases}
\label{sec:theory}

\begin{assumption}[Markov reader on bridge chains]\label{as:markov}
For bridge chains, $\pa(a\mid h_{1:k})$ depends on $h_{1:j}$ only through $h_{j+1:k}$ whenever
$h_{j+1}$ restates the result of $h_j$ ($j<k$).
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Single-step masking is a direct effect]\label{prop:direct}
$\Delta^{\mathrm{nai}}_j(a)$ equals the controlled direct effect of $H_j$ on $A$ with $H_{j+1:k}$
held at their observed values. Under \cref{as:markov}, $\Delta^{\mathrm{nai}}_j(a)=0$ for every
$j<k$ on bridge chains, whereas $\Delta^{\mathrm{tot}}_j(a)$ is not constrained to zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-baseline resampling is biased by the generator]\label{prop:rollout}
Let $q_j$ be the probability of a correct answer when hop $j$ is corrupted and the continuation is
resampled, and $r_j$ the same probability when hop $j$ is kept and the continuation is resampled.
On chains answered correctly with the original continuation ($\mathrm{PS}=1$), the
fixed-baseline estimator $1-q_j$ equals $(r_j-q_j)+(1-r_j)$: the resampled-baseline effect plus a
bias $1-r_j\ge0$ that does not depend on whether hop $j$ is necessary. For a redundant hop
($r_j=q_j$) it returns $1-r_j$ instead of $0$.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Remark.} The identifiability lemma of \citet{yu2025pns} assumes $P(A{=}y\mid\mathrm{do}(S))=1$,
i.e.\ $r_j=1$; \cref{prop:rollout} quantifies what happens when the rollout model cannot
regenerate a correct continuation. Thought-anchor style resampling~\citep{bogdan2025anchors}
uses a resampled baseline and is unbiased in this sense.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\paragraph{Data.} \textbf{CLUTRR} (\texttt{gen\_train234\_test2to10}), restricted to clean
simple-path stories (11{,}708 train / 635 test / validation), gives exact gold paths.
\textbf{MuSiQue-Ans} gives real text with gold decompositions; the context holds the supporting
paragraphs plus 4 distractors. MuSiQue was built to resist single-hop
shortcuts~\citep{min2019compositional}.

\paragraph{Estimator benchmark.} For each gold chain we insert one true but off-path hop
(necessity $0$) and score every hop with: mask hop only ($\Delta^{\text{nai}}$); mask hop plus DRG
descendants ($\Delta^{\text{tot}}$); counterfactual replacement of the hop's result without and with
propagation; resampling necessity with a fixed gold baseline and with a resampled baseline
($K=2$ samples per arm); and three controls (placebo rewrite, mask marker on the hop, mask markers
on the whole subtree). $\Delta$ is measured on the gold answer's probability normalised over the
candidate set and, as a robustness check that needs no candidate set, on its raw log-probability.
Per model we use 180 CLUTRR and 150 MuSiQue gold chains (about 2{,}800 scored hops including both
$T_{\text{copy}}$ variants); for Qwen2.5-7B, 120 and 100 chains and no resampling estimators.

\paragraph{Copy vs.\ compute.} $T_{\text{copy}}\in\{0,1\}$ appends to a CLUTRR gold chain one hop
that states the answer relation; everything else is identical. A second variant states a
\emph{wrong} relation that contradicts the relational hops; we report how often the model follows
it, separately for chains the model solves without the extra hop.

\paragraph{Input interventions and mediation.} \texttt{rename}, \texttt{order}, \texttt{distract}
(CLUTRR) and \texttt{dist8}, \texttt{order} (MuSiQue) have true effect $0$. For each question we
observe $Y(0,M(0))$ (original input, original chain), $Y(1,M(1))$ (intervened input, regenerated
chain) and $Y(1,M(0))$ (intervened input, \emph{original} chain fed back), giving the natural
direct effect $\mathrm{NDE}=\mathbb E[Y(1,M(0))-Y(0,M(0))]$ and the total indirect effect
$\mathrm{TIE}=\mathrm{TE}-\mathrm{NDE}$~\citep{pearl2001direct} without cross-world assumptions.

\paragraph{Models and statistics.} Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B-Instruct~\citep{qwen2025qwen25} and
SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct~\citep{allal2025smollm2}, fp16, greedy decoding, one Kaggle T4 each (7B
split over two). Mediation is run on 500 MuSiQue questions for Qwen2.5-1.5B and 3B, and on 120
for the two full decoding runs. Bootstrap 95\% CIs (2{,}000 resamples; for position means we
resample questions, not hops), paired bootstrap and exact McNemar tests.

\paragraph{R1: single-step masking misses bridge reasoning (\cref{prop:direct}).}
Wherever the answer is written in the chain (MuSiQue; CLUTRR with $T_{\text{copy}}$), masking one
non-final hop moves $p(\text{gold})$ by $-3.9$ to $+6.5$ points at every scale, while masking it with
its descendants moves it by $41$--$77$ points (\cref{tab:scale}). Total-effect estimators reach AUC
$0.86$--$0.98$ on MuSiQue and $0.94$--$1.00$ on CLUTRR+copy, direct-effect ones $0.64$--$0.82$
(\cref{tab:est}). The counterfactual-replacement implementation, which puts no mask on hop $j$,
and the raw log-probability version, which needs no candidate set, agree with the masking one.
The placebo control is at chance. The volume-matched control (mask tokens over the same subtree,
content kept) moves $p(\text{gold})$ by $-0.3$ to $11$ points against $41$--$77$ for the total
effect, so the effect is not an artefact of mask volume; it does carry some ranking signal on
CLUTRR+copy (AUC up to $0.95$ at 0.5B), which we report as a limitation of mask-based controls
for the smallest model.

\paragraph{R2: fixed-baseline resampling assigns necessity to redundant steps (\cref{prop:rollout}).}
On MuSiQue the fixed-baseline resampling estimator gives $56$--$78$ points of necessity to the
\emph{redundant} hop, indistinguishable from necessary hops ($61$--$79$; AUC $0.51$--$0.55$). By
\cref{prop:rollout} this is $1-r$: the models regenerate a correct continuation only about
$20$--$45\%$ of the time. With a resampled baseline the redundant hop drops to $-0.3$ to $4.3$
points, as the proposition predicts, and AUC rises to $0.61$--$0.67$. The remaining necessity of
gold hops under resampling is small ($-0.2$ to $11$ points) because the model re-derives a
corrupted step from the context: resampling measures whether a step is \emph{recoverable},
total-effect masking whether the written step is \emph{used}, and the fixed-baseline estimator
measures neither.

\paragraph{R3: chains are read, not reasoned over.} On CLUTRR the answer is a relation that must
be composed from the hops and never appears in them. The total effect of a non-final gold hop is
$-1.5$ to $1.8$ points up to 1.7B, $7.3$ [$3.8$, $11.0$] at 3B and $1.3$ [$-3.4$, $6.0$] at 7B,
an order of magnitude below the $52$--$77$ points the same hops carry once one hop stating the
answer is appended ($T_{\text{copy}}$, direct effect $0.9$--$1.7$). Is this because the models
cannot compose? The wrong-answer variant separates the two readings (\cref{tab:copywrong}). Up to
3B the stated wrong answer is followed on $46$--$67\%$ of questions, and \emph{as often on
questions the model solves from the plain chain} ($48$--$71\%$) as on those it does not: copying
overrides a solution the model can produce. Qwen2.5-7B follows it on $23\%$ overall but on only
$10\%$ [$3$, $21$] of the questions it solves, so at 7B a stated answer is checked against the
model's own composition---yet even there the relational hops carry no measurable effect, i.e.\
the composition does not run through the chain. Chains that reach every gold-path entity also do
not raise CoT accuracy (\cref{tab:readout}). At every scale we test, the dependence on the chain
tracks the readout the model can execute---copying a string---rather than the reasoning the chain
encodes.

\paragraph{R4: distractors act on chain-of-thought through the chain.} On 500 MuSiQue questions,
four extra distractor paragraphs change CoT exact match by $-2.6$ [$-6.2$, $1.2$] (Qwen-1.5B) and
$-5.6$ [$-9.6$, $-1.6$] (Qwen-3B). Feeding the original chain back under the distracted input
removes the effect (NDE $+1.0$ and $-0.6$, both CIs covering $0$); what remains goes through the
regenerated chain (TIE $-3.6$ [$-7.2$, $0.0$] and $-5.0$ [$-8.8$, $-1.0$]; \cref{tab:mediation}).
Direct answering shows the opposite split (NDE $-4.6$ and $-3.8$, TIE $\ge-2.0$), as does CAD
(NDE $-7.0$ and $-5.0$). The pilot on 120 questions overestimated the magnitude (TE $-9.2$, TIE
$-10.0$); the qualitative split replicates, the size is about $3$--$6$ points. CoT is not more
vulnerable than direct answering overall: the paired difference of total effects is $+3.0$
[$-0.8$, $6.8$] and $+0.2$ [$-3.8$, $4.2$]; nor is CAD ($-2.6$ [$-6.0$, $0.8$], $-1.6$
[$-4.4$, $1.2$]).

\paragraph{R5: a chain-level decoding probe cannot help, as R3--R4 predict.} We formulate
\hgcd{}, which adds $\alpha$ times the average total effect of the chain's answer-relevant hops to
each candidate's probability (\cref{app:method}). It can only amplify a dependence that exists and
acts after the chain is generated. Consistently, it equals CoT on both datasets and models
($|\Delta|\le1.7$ points, $p\ge0.6$). The rules that do beat CoT use no information from the
chain: content-free label calibration~\citep{zhao2021calibrate} ($+5.9$ on CLUTRR for Qwen-1.5B;
on MuSiQue with $n{=}500$, $+4.0$, $p{=}0.002$, and $+5.4$, $p<0.001$, for 1.5B and 3B), which is
itself fragile under a neutral-sentence placebo ($-5.0$ [$-10.1$, $-0.4$] for SmolLM2); and, at
1.5B, CAD on the direct readout ($+8.6$ over CoT, $p<0.001$; not at 3B). The mixture-reader
assumption behind our decoding propositions (\cref{app:method}) is not supported by these data.

\section{From diagnosis to decoding: execute the composition outside the model}
\label{sec:decoding}
R3--R5 locate the failure: the answer is read from the chain rather than computed over it, and
distractors act through chain generation. Two decoding interventions follow, and we tested both
against greedy free-form CoT and compute-matched self-consistency (SC), with every endpoint fixed in
\texttt{REVIEW.md} before the run that tests it.

\paragraph{Cutting the distractor $\to$ chain path does not help.} On MuSiQue we removed or reordered
paragraphs before the chain was generated, using the model's calibrated attention from the question
to each paragraph (calibrated as in ICR~\citealp{chen2025icr}) or the model's own selection (\cref{tab:musique-selection}).
Hard filtering keeps all gold paragraphs too rarely (top-4 recall $0.77$--$0.86$ at 3B), and a
lost gold paragraph halves accuracy; reranking that keeps every paragraph is neutral; self-selection
is worse. Nothing beats SC. The oracle that keeps only gold paragraphs gains $9$--$14$ EM, so the
distractor cost is real but not recoverable by these selectors at $\le3$B. (Attention scores
overflowed in fp16 for Qwen-1.5B and the sharded 7B; the 1.5B rows are from a corrected fp32 rerun,
7B attention results are excluded.)

\paragraph{Making the answer a function of the chain works.} On CLUTRR the model only
\emph{extracts} facts (``$X$'s \emph{rel} is $Y$'') and states each person's gender from the
story's pronouns; the facts form a kinship graph, and a composition table induced from the
\emph{training} split (126 rules; $95\%$ correct on gold chains) composes the relations along the
path from the query's source to its target, falling back to CoT when no path exists (in the spirit of
Faithful CoT~\citealp{lyu2023faithful}, but with the extractor and the rules separated). The structural
equations of the reasoning graph are thus executed rather than imitated. Chain-order parsing of the
model's own CoT applied to only $4$--$6\%$ of chains, and extraction without genders to
$12$--$18\%$; diagnosis showed that most failures were missing inverse edges, which need the gender
of the person they describe. With model-stated genders the method beats SC by $21$--$37$ points on
short chains at every scale, on the held-out validation split and on a CLUTRR task never used before
(\cref{tab:clutrr-symbolic}); on unseen long chains ($k{=}5$--8) it helps from 3B ($+9.5$) and
strongly at 7B ($+24.0$ [$+17.0$, $+31.5$], the pre-registered primary endpoint), but not at 1.5B,
whose extraction is too incomplete.

\paragraph{The gain is caused by the composition, not by the re-presented facts.} Holding the
extraction fixed, we let the model answer from its own extracted facts instead of the rule table
(\cref{tab:clutrr-ablation}). On short chains this is no better than plain CoT ($-3.2$ to $+1.3$),
while the rule table on the same facts is $13$--$35$ points better at every scale, and $13$ points
better at 7B on long chains: the information needed to answer is present in the model's output, and
the step that fails is composing it, exactly as R3 diagnosed.

\section{Limitations and scope}
\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Scale.} Our models are 0.5--7B. R1 and R2 are identities whose magnitude we confirm at every
scale. R3 changes at 7B in one respect (stated wrong answers are mostly vetoed) and not in the other
(relational hops still carry no effect); whether composition over the chain appears at 14B and in
other open families (OLMo-2) is the next experiment. The 7B run uses fewer chains and no
resampling estimators.
\textbf{Sample size.} The estimator benchmark scores about 2{,}800 hops per model and mediation uses
500 questions; the CLUTRR decoding comparisons use 238 questions, so accuracy differences below about
6 points there are not resolved. The wrong-copy follow rate on solvable questions rests on
$24$--$44$ questions per model (CIs in \cref{tab:copywrong}).
\textbf{Assumptions.} \cref{as:markov} idealises the reader; the data match its prediction. The
mixture assumption used for the decoding probe does not hold (R5).
\textbf{Masking.} Subtree masking inserts several mask tokens. Counterfactual replacement, the raw
log-probability effect and the volume-matched marker control agree with it in magnitude, but the
marker control is not inert at 0.5B (R1).
\textbf{Parsing.} The DRG is built from explicit mentions. On the models' own CLUTRR chains, format
success is $100\%$, gold-path entities are reached in $57\%$ (Qwen) / $60\%$ (SmolLM2) of hops, and
the DRG is fully connected in $25\%$ / $36\%$ of chains; this affects only the decoding probe, since
the estimator benchmark uses gold chains.
\textbf{Candidate sets.} Effects on MuSiQue are measured on the gold answer's probability over a
candidate set (gold recall $56\%$ before adding the model's strings); the raw log-probability
version, which needs no candidate set, gives the same picture (\cref{tab:est}).

\section{Conclusion}
With interventions whose ground truth is known, two common ways of measuring the importance of a
reasoning step turn out to be biased in predictable ways: single-step masking measures a direct
effect that vanishes on bridge chains, and fixed-baseline resampling adds the generator's failure
rate. Correctly measured, models from 0.5B to 7B depend on their chains when the answer can be read
from them, not when it must be computed; small models copy a stated answer even against their own
solution, and 7B vetoes it without composing through the chain either. Distractors hurt
chain-of-thought through the chain it regenerates. Decoding rules that act on the chain's readout
cannot repair either; executing the composition the chain describes, outside the model, can.

\section{The decoding probe in full}
\label{app:method}

\paragraph{Answer-level rule (L2).} With uniform weights $w_j=1/|\Rel(y)|$ on relevant hops and a
plausibility set $\mathcal{V}_\beta=\{a:\pa(a\mid y)\ge\beta\max_{a'}\pa(a'\mid y)\}$
\citep{li2023contrastive},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat a&=\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{V}_\beta}\; s_\alpha(a),\\
s_\alpha(a)&=(1+\alpha)\,\pa(a\mid y)-\alpha\!\!\sum_{j\in\Rel(y)}\!\!w_j\,\pa(a\mid\dotot{j})
=\pa(a\mid y)+\alpha\!\!\sum_{j\in\Rel(y)}\!\!w_j\,\Delta^{\mathrm{tot}}_j(a).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hgcd}
\end{equation}
The score is the answer probability plus $\alpha$ times the average total effect of the
answer-relevant hops on that answer. Redundant hops ($j\notin\Rel(y)$) are never contrasted.

\paragraph{Evidence-level rule (L1).} While emitting the \emph{result} tokens of a hop, we contrast
the real context $P$ with a type-matched counterfactual $\tilde P$ of identical length and entity
types in which the relational content differs (on CLUTRR: names permuted within each gender
group, so every sentence and entity type is kept but who-is-related-to-whom changes; we evaluate
L1 on CLUTRR only, where $\tilde P$ is exactly type-matched):
$t^\star=\arg\max_{t\in\mathcal V_\beta}(1{+}\alpha)\pa(t\mid P,\cdot)-\alpha\pa(t\mid\tilde P,\cdot)$.
Unlike an empty context, $\tilde P$ is in-distribution and controls for length and type cues.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\hgcd{} (L2) for one question}
\label{alg:hgcd}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $y\gets$ greedy chain from the model (optionally with L1 contrast on result tokens)
\State Parse hops; build DRG; compute relevant set $\Rel(y)$
\State $p\gets\pa(\cdot\mid Q,P,y)$ over $\mathcal{A}$ \Comment{one forward + KV-cached candidates}
\For{$j\in\Rel(y)$} \State $p_j\gets\pa(\cdot\mid Q,P,\dotot{j})$ \EndFor
\State \Return $\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{V}_\beta}(1+\alpha)p(a)-\alpha\,\mathrm{mean}_j\,p_j(a)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{remark}[What each level can fix]
L2 makes the answer depend \emph{more} on the chain; it therefore repairs \emph{readout} errors
(chain correct, answer wrong because a shortcut or distractor dominates the readout) but cannot
repair a wrong chain---it may even commit harder to one. Chain errors are the target of L1, which
acts while the hops are produced. 
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Cost.} L2 needs $1+|\Rel(y)|$ prefix forwards and a KV-cached pass over the candidate
tokens; no extra generation. L1 doubles the forward cost of result tokens only.
\paragraph{Choice of $\alpha$.} $\alpha$ is selected on the CLUTRR validation split and frozen
(ties broken toward the smaller $\alpha$, so \hgcd{} reduces to CoT absent evidence); an
adaptive variant sets $\alpha\cdot\mathrm{JSD}(p,\bar p_j)$ as in AdaCAD~\citep{wang2025adacad}.

\subsection*{Propositions for the decoding probe}
These propositions rest on a mixture-reader assumption that our data do not support (R5); we keep
them because they make the probe's intended mechanism explicit.

\begin{assumption}[Mixture reader]\label{as:mixture}
$\pa(a\mid Q,P,y)=\lambda_c\pa_c(a\mid y,P_{\mathrm{sup}})+\lambda_s\pa_s(a\mid Q)+
\lambda_d\pa_d(a\mid P_{\mathrm{dist}})$, with $\lambda$'s non-negative, summing to one, and
invariant to interventions on hops.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Exact cancellation in probability space]\label{prop:mixture}
Under \cref{as:mixture},
\[
s_\alpha(a)=\pa(a\mid y)+\alpha\lambda_c\sum_j w_j\big[\pa_c(a\mid y)-\pa_c(a\mid\dotot{j})\big],
\]
i.e.\ the shortcut $\pa_s$ and distractor $\pa_d$ components cancel in the contrast for every
$\alpha$. The log-space analogue does not cancel them.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[CAD amplifies distractor paths]\label{prop:cad}
Under \cref{as:mixture} applied to direct answering, with no-context distribution $\pa_s(a\mid Q)$,
CAD's score is $(1{+}\alpha)\log\big[\lambda_c\pa_c+\lambda_s\pa_s+\lambda_d\pa_d\big](a)-
\alpha\log\pa_s(a\mid Q)$. For any $\alpha>0$ and any gold answer $g$, a distractor answer $a_d$ with
$\pa_d(a_d)=1$ is preferred by CAD whenever $\pa_s(a_d\mid Q)$ is small enough, even if
$\pa(g\mid Q,P)>\pa(a_d\mid Q,P)$. In contrast, \hgcd{}'s contrast term does not depend on
$\pa_d$ at all (\cref{prop:mixture}).
\end{proposition}
\emph{Prediction.} Under distractor injection, CAD's accuracy degrades more than \hgcd{}'s, i.e.\
$|\mathrm{ATE}_{\mathrm{distract}}|$ is larger for CAD.

\begin{proposition}[PNS limit]\label{prop:pns}
Interpret $\pa(a\mid\dotot{j})$ as $P(A{=}a\mid\mathrm{do}(H_j{=}\bot))$ and assume exogeneity and
monotonicity as in \citet{yu2025pns}. Then $\Delta^{\mathrm{tot}}_j(a)=\mathrm{PNS}_j(a)$ and
$\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}s_\alpha(a)/\alpha=\sum_{j\in\Rel(y)}w_j\,\mathrm{PNS}_j(a)$. Hence, within
$\mathcal{V}_\beta$, \hgcd{} interpolates between the most probable answer ($\alpha{=}0$) and the
answer for which the relevant hops are, on average, most necessary and sufficient.
\end{proposition}

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\paragraph{\Cref{prop:direct}.} $\donai{j}$ changes only $h_j$ and keeps $h_{j+1:k}$ at their
observed values, so $\Delta^{\mathrm{nai}}_j$ is by definition the controlled direct effect
$\pa(a\mid h_j,h_{-j})-\pa(a\mid\bot,h_{-j})$~\citep{pearl2001direct}. Under \cref{as:markov},
$\pa(a\mid h_{1:k})=g(a\mid h_{j+1:k})$ for $j<k$, so both terms equal $g(a\mid h_{j+1:k})$ and the
difference is zero. $\dotot{j}$ additionally changes $h_{j+1:k}$ (their results are masked), so
$g$ is evaluated at a different argument and the difference is unconstrained. On compositional
chains $\pa$ reads the relation of every hop, so removing $h_j$ alone changes the composed relation.
$\square$

\paragraph{\Cref{prop:mixture}.} Substituting \cref{as:mixture} into \cref{eq:hgcd}, with
$\sum_j w_j=1$ and $\pa_s,\pa_d$ unaffected by interventions on hops:
$s_\alpha(a)=(1{+}\alpha)[\lambda_c\pa_c(a\mid y)+\lambda_s\pa_s+\lambda_d\pa_d]-
\alpha\sum_j w_j[\lambda_c\pa_c(a\mid\dotot{j})+\lambda_s\pa_s+\lambda_d\pa_d]
=\pa(a\mid y)+\alpha\lambda_c\sum_jw_j[\pa_c(a\mid y)-\pa_c(a\mid\dotot{j})]$.
In log space, $\log(\lambda_c u+r)-\log(\lambda_c v+r)$ depends on $r=\lambda_s\pa_s+\lambda_d\pa_d$
unless $u=v$, so the shortcut terms do not cancel. $\square$

\paragraph{\Cref{prop:cad}.} Let $m(a)=\pa(a\mid Q,P)$. CAD prefers $a_d$ over $g$ iff
$(1{+}\alpha)\log\frac{m(a_d)}{m(g)}>\alpha\log\frac{\pa_s(a_d)}{\pa_s(g)}$. The left side is
bounded below by $(1{+}\alpha)\log\frac{\lambda_d}{m(g)}$ since $m(a_d)\ge\lambda_d$, while the right
side tends to $-\infty$ as $\pa_s(a_d)\to0$. For \hgcd{}, both terms of \cref{eq:hgcd} condition
on the same $P$, so $\lambda_d\pa_d$ enters both and cancels (\cref{prop:mixture}). $\square$

\paragraph{\Cref{prop:pns}.} Under exogeneity and monotonicity, Lemma 2 of \citet{yu2025pns}
gives $\mathrm{PNS}=P(A{=}a\mid\mathrm{do}(S))-P(A{=}a\mid\mathrm{do}(S'))$. With $S=y$ and $S'=\dotot{j}$
this is $\Delta^{\mathrm{tot}}_j(a)$. Dividing \cref{eq:hgcd} by $\alpha$ and letting
$\alpha\to\infty$ removes the $\pa(a\mid y)/\alpha$ term. $\square$

\section{Implementation and Reproducibility}
\label{app:impl}
Candidate log-probabilities are computed with one prefix forward and a KV-cached pass over the
candidate tokens (verified against full recomputation to $2\times10^{-5}$). Prompts are two-shot,
hop-structured; the second CLUTRR shot uses a relation outside the label set to avoid leaking label
statistics. All interventions are seeded functions of the example id. The Kaggle notebook embeds
the full package and writes every table in this paper.
